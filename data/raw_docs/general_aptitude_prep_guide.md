# General Campus Placement Preparation — Aptitude and Reasoning

## Introduction

Most campus placement drives at technology companies begin with an online aptitude
and reasoning assessment. This document covers the topics most commonly tested across
NovaTech, Meridian FinTech, Aether Robotics, and similar companies, along with
problem-solving strategies and typical time constraints.

## Quantitative Aptitude

### Number Systems and Arithmetic
- Divisibility rules, HCF and LCM, prime factorisation.
- Percentage calculations: successive discounts, profit-and-loss percentage, simple
  and compound interest.
- Ratio and proportion: direct and inverse variation, mixture and alligation.

**Time management tip**: Number-theory problems appear simple but can be time-consuming.
Skip and return if a problem takes more than 90 seconds.

### Algebra
- Linear equations with two unknowns.
- Quadratic equations: factorisation and the quadratic formula.
- Arithmetic and geometric progressions: nth-term formula, sum of n terms.

### Work, Time, and Distance
- Work-rate problems: combined rates, partial completion.
- Distance-speed-time: relative speed, circular tracks, train-passing-pole problems.

**Common mistake**: forgetting to unify units (mixing km/h and m/s) when computing
relative speed or time.

### Permutations and Combinations
- Counting arrangements of distinct and identical objects.
- Selection with and without repetition.
- Circular arrangements.

### Probability
- Classical probability, mutually exclusive events, independent events.
- Conditional probability and Bayes' theorem (may appear as a single MCQ in some drives).

## Logical Reasoning

### Syllogisms
- Venn diagram method for drawing valid conclusions from two or more premises.
- Distinguishing "definitely true", "possibly true", and "false" conclusions.

### Blood Relations and Direction Sense
- Decoding family trees from coded statements.
- Net displacement and compass-direction problems.

### Series Completion
- Number series: arithmetic, geometric, and mixed patterns.
- Letter series: alphabetical position-based patterns.
- Figure series: rotation, reflection, counting of segments.

### Statement-Conclusion and Statement-Assumption
- Identifying assumptions implicit in a given argument.
- Evaluating whether a conclusion follows logically from the given premises.

### Data Interpretation
- Reading bar charts, line graphs, pie charts, and tables.
- Multi-step calculations: percentage growth, ratio comparisons, value extrapolation.

**Study tip**: Practice with datasets that have five or more questions per set; the
bottleneck is speed, not comprehension.

## Verbal Ability

Many drives (including NovaTech's) include a verbal or English-proficiency section
not listed in the headline format. Common question types:

- Reading comprehension: 150–250 word passage, 3–4 questions on main idea, inference,
  and vocabulary.
- Error correction: identifying grammatical errors in sentences.
- Para-jumbles: rearranging shuffled sentences into a coherent paragraph.
- Vocabulary: synonyms, antonyms, fill-in-the-blank with appropriate word choice.

## Online Assessment Strategy

### Day-of-Assessment Checklist
1. Join at least 10 minutes before the scheduled start time; confirm webcam and
   microphone (proctoring is common).
2. Read instructions for each section fully before starting that section.
3. Attempt questions you are confident about first; flag uncertain ones for review.
4. In the aptitude section, use estimation and elimination to narrow options quickly
   rather than computing precise answers where approximation suffices.
5. For coding sections: write a brute-force solution first to earn partial credit,
   then optimise within remaining time.

### Common Mistakes to Avoid
- Spending more than 2 minutes on a single aptitude MCQ.
- Not reading all options before selecting the first plausible one (distractors are
  often designed to be attractive).
- Submitting code on the first run without testing on the provided example input.
- Forgetting to handle edge cases in coding problems (empty array, n=0, negative numbers).

## Recommended Practice Resources

- **Aptitude**: IndiaBix, PrepInsta topic-wise practice sets.
- **Reasoning**: RS Aggarwal "A Modern Approach to Verbal and Non-Verbal Reasoning".
- **Coding**: LeetCode (filter by Easy and Medium, focus on Arrays, Strings, Trees, Graphs).
- **CS Fundamentals**: GeeksForGeeks topic articles for DBMS, OS, CN, and OOP.
